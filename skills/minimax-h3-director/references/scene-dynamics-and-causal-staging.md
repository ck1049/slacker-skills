# Scene dynamics, reference roles, and causal staging

Classification: `project_experiment` design guidance derived from production failures; not an official H3 capability guarantee. Keep the bundled official field grammar, labels, asset limits and platform constraints unchanged. Use the relevant checks when secondary participants, crowds, environmental affordances or device interactions matter to the story; a quiet single-subject shot need not acquire an elaborate crowd.

## Build a complete scene before compiling a prompt

Separate the main event from supporting activity. Give consequential secondary actors an intention and response. Describe ordinary NPCs as a few behavior groups: near pedestrians crossing a sightline, a middle queue advancing in short steps, far walkers with slower displacement. Specify direction, occlusion and contact where they affect the event. Do not give every extra a new asset, speaking role or synchronized motion.

In a crowd-pressure shot, a close shoulder/bag contact should cause a support-foot transfer, knee flexion or recovery step, with clothing following. Stable paving or rail landmarks help distinguish physical displacement from a camera pan. Continuous low-level crowd movement can continue during dialogue. A protected empty circle around the leads contradicts crowd pressure unless the story explains it. Background motion is not mandatory in deliberately still scenes or a clearly edited photograph hold.

An emergency corridor uses another mechanism: a nurse's cart crosses a visitor's route, the visitor draws in their feet, and corridor sound enters through an opening door. Do not transplant tourist jostling into every group scene. A reading room may remain nearly still, with occasional page turns and restrained room tone.

Write these as positive observable actions in `detailed_description`; describe the corresponding spatial sound in `overall_soundscape`. Keep planning tables outside the copy-ready block. Preserve English rewrite sections and original-language dialogue/lyrics inside `<d>`, or explicitly described visible scene text. Do not place Chinese plot summaries in rewrite prose and then treat narration as a model-only failure.

## Check scene affordances and reference content

If the characters search for a seat, exit, shelter or useful tool, check what is already visible, reachable and available. A nearby empty bench undermines a search for somewhere to rest. Reveal a distant available recess with a motivated view and legible connecting route, then show movement or a clear editorial cut that compresses the journey. Occupied or inaccessible furniture may be plausible if the image communicates why it cannot solve the problem.

Distinguish identity references, environment references and composition/timed guides. A setting image containing the protagonists may copy their poses or create extra protagonists. A generic NPC with the hero's signature braid/cape can also be mistaken for that hero. Inspect the actual reference and repair ambiguity, rather than only adding negative wording. An environment reference need not lock an entire frame, and a first-frame guide is not evidence that the subsequent action occurs.

Reuse a reviewed actual state where continuity requires it, not the same full layout indiscriminately across an episode. A new over-shoulder view needs a suitable composition or motivated camera transition. Failed upstream output remains diagnostic evidence and cannot become an accepted dependency merely because its last frame exists.

## Separate operation direction from viewing direction

For a device action specify operator/support, hand contact, working face, target and result. State whether the observer sees the front, side or back. A raised camera pointed at the viewer cannot photograph companions beside or behind its owner; a through-camera shot excludes that camera's body from its own picture. The same check applies to lamps, spray nozzles and scanning windows.

Repeated orientation/contact failures call for a readable side/back guide, a turning/contact keyframe, reduced competing actions or an authorized edit. More sampling steps and longer prohibition lists cannot repair a contradictory geometry. A tripod, delay mechanism or helper is an optional story choice, not a universal fix or an automatically canonical feature.

Only bind guides using verified capabilities of the actual execution surface. Native timed Guide nodes, hosted reference inputs and first/last-frame modes have different contracts. Validate the graph and asset bindings separately from the official-format prompt. Increased steps should use a compatible workflow; do not claim that changing a step count proves better motion.

## Sound, snapshot timing and review

Plan continuous unintelligible crowd ambience, localized steps/cloth and important discrete sounds separately. Preserve spatial transitions: nearby crowd becomes distant beyond an arch/door while water or room tone becomes clearer. Score is optional and follows the user's intent; licensed local music and foley need sources, attribution where required, dialogue ducking and event timing.

Before adding a shutter/bell/hit, check the actual native audio and triggering action to avoid duplication. Choose a snapshot after complete dialogue and a verified expression, then use an explicitly recorded local hold if needed. Keep original dialogue and frame-duration accounting. ASR or waveform inspection does not certify hearing, timbre, lip sync or sound layers.

Record design checks, file/graph checks, observed frames, continuous viewing, listening and user acceptance separately. Review the main event plus consequential NPC behavior, contact, available space, working-face direction and adjacent state. A plausible still frame is not a successful motion sample. Stop real downstream dependencies of a failed core event, retain independent valid work, and change an execution-relevant variable before retrying.
